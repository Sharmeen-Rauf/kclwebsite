## Airspace Transit vs Aerodrome Touchdown

When planning international flight operations, one of the primary regulatory distinctions dispatchers encounter is the requirement for an **overflight permit** versus a **landing permit**. While both represent sovereign civil authorizations issued under international air law, their operational scope, documentation requirements, and coordination workflows differ significantly.

## What Is an Overflight Permit?

An overflight permit authorizes an aircraft to enter, transit, and exit the sovereign airspace and Flight Information Regions (FIRs) of a foreign state without landing at an aerodrome within that state.

- **Airspace Scope:** Confined strictly to designated ATS airways, flight levels, and FIR boundary entry and exit waypoints.
- **Key Operator Details:** Complete route string, entry and exit coordinates with estimated times (ETO), aircraft registration and type, MTOW, and callsign.
- **Coordination Profile:** Handled directly between the designated flight permission agent or operator and the national civil aviation authority (CAA) alongside military or defense liaison where required.

## What Is a Landing Permit?

A landing permit grants permission for an international aircraft to touch down at a specific civil or joint-user aerodrome within foreign sovereign territory.

- **Operational Purposes:** Commercial traffic stops (embarking or disembarking passengers/cargo), technical refueling stops, crew changeovers, maintenance positioning, or medical evacuations.
- **Extended Documentation:** In addition to route and aircraft specs, landing applications require consignee/charterer details, local ground handling confirmations, airport slot allocations (at coordinated aerodromes), and passenger manifests or dangerous goods declarations.
- **Station Synchronisation:** A landing permit must remain synchronized with apron readiness, ramp handling availability, fueling contracts, and crew accommodation.

## Lead Times and Filing Differences

Overflight clearances often follow standardized lead times—typically between 24 and 72 working hours depending on the FIR and whether defense review is required. In contrast, landing clearances frequently involve inter-agency coordination between civil aviation regulators, airport security, customs and border control, and slot coordination committees.

## Summary Checklist for Dispatchers

1. **Verify the Route:** Determine every FIR along your route string where an active overflight authorization number must be entered in Field 18 of the ICAO Flight Plan.
2. **Align Ground Handlers Early:** Never file for a landing permit without confirmed local handling coordination at the destination airport.
3. **Monitor Validity Windows:** Confirm whether the granted permit allows a 24-hour, 48-hour, or 72-hour operating window around your planned UTC schedule.
