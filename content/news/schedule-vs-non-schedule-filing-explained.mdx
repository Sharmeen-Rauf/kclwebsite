## Why Filing Classification Matters Operationally

When preparing an overflight or landing permission request for Pakistan, the first classification decision is whether the movement is a **scheduled flight** or a **non-scheduled flight**. Each category involves distinct documentation packages, authority review procedures, and revision workflows.

## What Qualifies as Scheduled Flight Permission Filing?

Scheduled filings apply to commercial passenger and all-cargo airlines operating published timetables across the IATA Northern Summer or Northern Winter seasons.

- **Typical Mission Profile:** Recurring commercial airline routes transiting `OPKR` and `OPLR` FIRs or making regular traffic stops at Pakistani international aerodromes.
- **Operator Preparation:** A complete seasonal timetable listing flight designators, days of operation, effective dates, aircraft types, entry/exit waypoints, and UTC timings, supported by fleet-wide airworthiness and insurance certificates.
- **Ongoing Season Management:** Coordinating registration additions when newly delivered or leased aircraft join the fleet and submitting timetable adjustments during the season.

## What Qualifies as Non-Scheduled Flight Permission Filing?

Non-scheduled filings cover single-sector or short-series flights operated outside a published seasonal airline timetable.

- **Typical Mission Profile:** Ad-hoc passenger or cargo charters, business aviation flights, aircraft ferry and delivery sectors, medical evacuation (medevac) missions, and technical refueling stops.
- **Operator Preparation:** A mission-specific package detailing operator identity, callsign, primary and backup tail numbers, MTOW, complete route string, flight purpose, and UTC schedule.
- **Operational Focus:** Responsive 24/7 follow-up for schedule shifts and close alignment with aircraft ground handling, aviation fuel coordination, and crew logistics.

## Confirming the Appropriate Path Before You File
